# Assignment 1. What is something you believe to be true that most people around you would disagree with?

### Rules

* No "safe" answers.
* Must be your real belief (not copied from internet).
* Minimum 50 words.

**Hint:** What do you believe about career, money, learning, discipline, relationships, health, success, life, tech industry, etc. that most people don't agree with?

## Answer

I believe that certifications and technical platform credentials are mere baseline signals, not true differentiators or proof of expertise. Most people in the tech ecosystem blindly believe that accumulating cloud badges or memorizing product documentation guarantees career mobility and structural engineering capability. I disagree entirely.

True expertise lies in systemic, architectural mastery. The ability to automate variable contexts, anticipate cascading system failures, and treat infrastructure strictly as modular software. A professional with zero active platform certifications who deeply understands continuous delivery loops, networking constraints, and automated security boundaries will always out-engineer a highly certified individual who relies on manual point-and-click operations with a cloud GUI portal. Out-learning the crowd requires abandoning the pursuit of mere paper signals and committing to raw execution and public proof of work.

### Definition

**Evidence from my life:** (2–4 lines: what you tried + what happened)

---

## Truth #1

### Truth

Clicking buttons in a web dashboard to configure enterprise systems always invites human error.

### Evidence from my life

In M365 support, I watched admins manually copy settings from test to production, miss a single checkbox, and accidentally crash mail flow or lock out hundreds of users. It proved that human clicks don't scale.

---

## Truth #2

### Truth

Trying to add security to a cloud setup after it is already built always breaks things.

### Evidence from my life

I handled endless panic tickets where turning on MFA or Conditional Access late in the game completely shattered a company's existing app workflows. Security boundaries must be there on day one.

---

## Truth #3

### Truth

You cannot fix a complex system failure by guessing settings; you have to map the data flow.

### Evidence from my life

Whenever a hybrid identity sync failed, changing random UI settings just made things worse. The breakthrough only happened when I stepped back and drew a physical map of how the authentication tokens were traveling.

* etc.

**What emotion did you feel?** (minimum 50–100 words)

## Answer

Choosing to implement a rapid, superficial patch inside a system GUI to quickly silence an operational alert, rather than tracking down the true root cause within the configuration files, immediately triggered a profound sense of anxiety and internal professional stress. Even though the application appeared functional to external users, I felt an underlying guilt and shame because I knew the solution lacked structural engineering integrity. # Reflection – Week 1

### Biggest insight I got about myself this week

I realized that while my enterprise cloud administration background provides an exceptional foundation, my ultimate evolution into an elite DevOps engineer requires moving completely away from manual management console screens into an automated, systematic paradigm. True growth requires applying software design discipline to environment setups, relying on principles over opinion, and looking at progress through monthly compounding rather than daily emotions.

### My biggest weakness/loop I noticed

My most prevalent loop is the instinct to immediately jump into a cloud portal interface and debug a configuration error manually when a deployment fails, rather than stepping back to programmatically adjust the core declarative template or fully trace the network loop logic down to its abstract root causes.

### One system I will implement from this week (exact habit + time)

I will execute an uninterrupted 90-minute engineering focus block every single Monday through Friday from 05:00 AM to 06:30 AM, solely dedicated to building, testing, and debugging infrastructure configuration completely within my terminal and IDE.
